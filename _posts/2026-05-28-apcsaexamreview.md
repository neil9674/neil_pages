## Overview

Preparing for the AP Computer Science A exam was a process that pushed me to move beyond surface-level understanding and actually think like a programmer under pressure. Between AP Classroom progress checks, practice exams, FRQ submissions, and supplementary YouTube review videos, I built up my skills incrementally over the course of the semester.

---

## What the Data Shows

Looking at my progress check scores across Units 1–4, there's a clear story of early confidence, a dip when the material got harder, and a recovery as I adjusted my approach. Unit 1 came naturally — the foundations of Java syntax and primitive types weren't new territory. Unit 2 was humbling; working with objects and classes exposed gaps in how I was thinking about scope and method behavior. Rather than moving on and hoping it clicked, I went back and watched focused YouTube walkthroughs on those specific concepts, which made a noticeable difference in how I approached Unit 3 and beyond.

The jump from Practice Exam 1 to Practice Exam 2 MCQ wasn't dramatic, but it was meaningful — it confirmed that the extra review was translating into real performance, not just familiarity.

---

## The FRQ Side

The FRQ submissions were a different kind of challenge. Writing complete, syntactically correct Java solutions from scratch — without autocomplete or a compiler — forced me to slow down and be precise in a way that MCQs don't require. Submitting FRQs for Units 1, 2, and 4, as well as the Practice Exam 2 FRQ, gave me repeated reps at structuring methods, handling edge cases, and communicating logic clearly in code.

---

## YouTube as a Study Tool

One study habit that genuinely helped was watching YouTube review videos for each AP CSA unit. Reading through the CollegeBoard material can feel abstract, but seeing someone walk through a 2D array traversal or explain recursion with a concrete example made concepts click faster. I used these videos not as a replacement for practice, but as a way to reframe topics I was already working through — especially for the units I found less intuitive.

---

## Takeaway

My preparation wasn't perfect, but it was deliberate. I didn't just take the assessments and move on — I used lower scores as signals to go back and actually fix the gaps. That iterative cycle of assess, review, and retry is what I'm most proud of heading into the exam.
